PARDA: A Perceive-Act-Reflect-Distill Agent for Source-Free Unsupervised Domain Adaptation

Abstract

Source-Free Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (SFUDA) has emerged as a critical paradigm for addressing severe domain shifts in medical image segmentation under strict data privacy constraints, where source data are inaccessible. However, existing SFUDA methods primarily rely on static, open-loop adaptation pipelines, lacking the ability to actively perceive target domain characteristics and self-reflect to correct accumulated errors. Meanwhile, Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) and Vision Language Models (VLMs) exhibit strong generalization across modalities and anatomies, yet their high computational inference overhead and deployment constraints prevent direct adoption on clinical edge devices. In this work, we propose PARDA, a Perceive-Act-Reflect-Distill Agent for SFUDA in medical image segmentation. PARDA reformulates SFUDA as a closed-loop decision-making process, in which the agent actively perceives target domain characteristics, acts by coordinating semantic and geometric VFMs to generate pseudo-labels, reflects on the reliability of its anatomical and structural representations to guide refinement, and finally distills the acquired adaptation knowledge into a lightweight student model. We conduct the most comprehensive SFUDA evaluation to date, covering 13 cross-modality domain adaptation directions and 24 anatomy targets across diverse organs and imaging modalities. Experimental results demonstrate that PARDA consistently outperforms SOTA SFUDA methods, achieving robust and universal domain adaptation while maintaining a lightweight model for real-world clinical deployment.

Method

Perception

Actively characterizes target domain properties and dynamically plans appropriate pseudo-labeling strategies, enabling domain-aware adaptation.

Action

Coordinates semantic (BiomedParse) and geometric (SAM3) VFMs through a dual-expert strategy to generate high-quality pseudo-labels.

Reflection

VLM-driven semantic quality assessment that examines predictions, identifies unreliable pseudo-labels, and triggers targeted refinement.

Distillation

Transfers the agent's adaptation knowledge into a lightweight student model for efficient clinical edge device deployment.

Detailed illustration of PARDA. (a) The agent autonomously performs Perception, Action, and Reflection to obtain reliable pseudo-labels, followed by knowledge distillation into a lightweight model. (b) Perception criteria for characterizing target domain conditions. (c) Reflection criteria for semantic quality assessment.

Key Features

Perception-Guided Dual-Expert Action

PARDA tightly couples Perception with Action. The agent analyzes target domain characteristics (contrast, noise, difficulty) and dynamically selects strategies:

  • Single-expert — For simple scenarios, BiomedParse directly generates pseudo-labels
  • Dual-expert — For challenging scenarios, BiomedParse proposes semantics + SAM3 refines geometry

Reflection-Driven State-Dependent Refinement

The VLM assesses pseudo-label quality through multi-dimensional reasoning:

  • Anatomical plausibility — Whether the prediction matches known anatomy
  • Boundary precision — Quality of segmentation boundaries
  • Morphological consistency — Shape and structural coherence
  • False positive detection — Identifying spurious predictions

When rejected, adaptive repair tools are selected: Tzoom Tclahe Ttopo Tsym
